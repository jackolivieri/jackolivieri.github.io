Chart 1: Average Spend by Season and Payment Method

avg_by_season = df.groupby(["Season", "Payment_Method"])["Total_Cost"].mean().reset_index()

plt.figure(figsize=(8, 5))
sns.barplot(data=avg_by_season, x="Season", y="Total_Cost", hue="Payment_Method")
plt.title("Average Transaction Cost by Season and Payment Method")
plt.ylabel("Average Total Cost ($)")
plt.xlabel("Season")
plt.show()
  1. We can see that the average transaction cost stays flat across the four seasons and different payment methods.
  2. I chose a bar chart because it makes things like dollar averages easy to compare. The coloring helps distinguish between different payment methods. This helped make it easy to compare payment methods.
  3. Because the bars are so close in height, small differences are hard to spot at a glance.

Chart 2: Average Spend by Hour of Day

df["hour"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time_of_day"], format="%H:%M:%S").dt.hour

hourly = df.groupby("hour").agg(
    avg_cost=("Total_Cost", "mean"),
    n_transactions=("Transaction_ID", "count")
).reset_index()

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 5))
plt.scatter(hourly["hour"], hourly["avg_cost"], s=hourly["n_transactions"] / 5, alpha=0.6)
plt.title("Average Spend by Hour of Day (bubble size = number of transactions)")
plt.xlabel("Hour of Day (24h)")
plt.ylabel("Average Total Cost ($)")
plt.show()
  1. We can see that the average transaction cost stays fairly steady with no real big outliers. The bubble size shows that there are larger transactions later in the day.
  2. The x and y axes show the trend between the cost and time of day. The bubble size also shows how busy individual hours are without having to use another chart. This made it easy to see if there were any particularly high traffic times.
  3. The bubble size here can be hard to interpret just by looking at the chart. Perhaps adding a toolkit here that would make the chart more interactive would be better for future analysis.

Chart 3: Average Items per Basket by Season and Customer Category

avg_items = df.groupby(["Season", "Customer_Category"])["Total_Items"].mean().reset_index()

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 5))
sns.lineplot(data=avg_items, x="Season", y="Total_Items", hue="Customer_Category", marker="o")
plt.title("Average Items per Basket by Season and Customer Category")
plt.xlabel("Season")
plt.ylabel("Average Items per Basket")
plt.show()
  1. We can see there are no flat patterns across groups and that different customer segments act differently across seasons. For example, Teenagers and Young Adults start highest in Fall, then drop through Spring, while Seniors do the opposite.
  2. The axes make the chart easy to read and interpret how a group’s basket size changes with the seasons. Like in the first chart, color is used to separate and distinguish between different customer segments.
  3. There are a lot of converging and overlapping lines that could make this chart hard to read at a glance.
